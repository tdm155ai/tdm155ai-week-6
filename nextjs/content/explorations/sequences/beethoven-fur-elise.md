# Beethoven's Für Elise: sketches, a lost score, and a printing

## The story

*Für Elise* (WoO 59) is a short piano piece in A minor. Beethoven never published it.

The first trace is a 16-bar sketch of the melody from 1808, in a sketchbook now in Berlin (Landsberg 10) [3]. In 1810 he worked the piece out in ink on a bifolium now in Bonn (BH 116). The same sheet carries sketches for his *Egmont* music and a march [2]. He then wrote out a clean copy. Ludwig Nohl, who said he had seen it, reported the inscription "Für Elise am 27 April [1810] zur Erinnerung von L. v. Bthvn." That autograph is lost [1, 2].

Around 1822, Beethoven revisited earlier short piano pieces with publication in mind. Beethoven-Haus dates the numbering ‘No 12’ to that review and says the Elise sketches were reworked in 1822 [2]. It also explicitly identifies the pencil jottings on BH 116 as sketches for the other works on the sheet. The displayed detail is therefore not evidence of pencil revisions to Elise. The planned collection did not include the piece in this form [4].

Nohl printed the piece in 1867, in *Neue Briefe Beethovens* [1]. Who "Elise" was is still argued. Candidates include Therese Malfatti and the singer Elisabeth Röckel [1].

## Plan or practice?

Planning, but stretched over fourteen years. The 1808 sketch catches a tune among other ideas. The 1810 draft builds the whole piece. The later review returned to a draft for a publication that did not happen.

The odd thing is the ending. The version everyone plays comes from a lost score known only through Nohl's printing. The surviving draft and its later reworking need to be distinguished from the lost autograph and the 1867 printing.
