# Forsythe's Improvisation Technologies

**Wall label**

How do you teach improvisation? William Forsythe, director of Ballett Frankfurt, broke it into small operations with plain names: "point-point line," "room writing," "U-ing and O-ing." In short videos he demonstrates one operation at a time. Animated white lines trace the shapes his limbs imply in the air. The project began at the ZKM in Karlsruhe in 1994 as a training tool for his dancers. It came out on CD-ROM in 1999. When the disc stopped running on modern computers, the ZKM rebuilt it as a free website (2023). Each clip is a drill: learn one move, repeat it, then combine.

## What it is

A set of lecture-demonstrations, first published as *William Forsythe: Improvisation Technologies — A Tool for the Analytical Dance Eye* (CD-ROM and booklet, Hatje Cantz / ZKM, 1999) [1]. Dancers Christine Bürkle, Noah D. Gelber, Thomas McManus and Crystal Pite also demonstrate. The disc included Forsythe's *Solo*, filmed in 1995 by Thomas Lovell Balogh [1].

The website version groups lectures under **Lines**, **Writing**, **Reorganizing** and **Additions**, with titles such as Point-Point Line, Avoidance, Rotating Inscription, Room Writing, Spatial Reorientation, Compression and Isometries [2].

## What it was for

The ZKM describes the goal as a training tool that lets dancers understand and study Forsythe's movement principles on their own [3]. New company members needed to learn a way of improvising that the company had built up over years. The videos isolate one operation at a time, so a dancer can practice it until it is automatic, then use it inside a full improvisation.

## Look closely

- **Named operations.** Each lecture has a short, concrete name. Naming a move makes it repeatable and teachable.
- **Lines over the body.** The artist Paul Kaiser proposed tracing the "invisible geometry" of Forsythe's movement in the computer and laying it over the video; a first prototype was built at the ZKM in 1994 by Seth Goldstein, Christian Ziegler, Volker Kuchelmeister and Mohsen Hosseini [3].
- **Counting is inconsistent.** The ZKM's publication page says 60 video chapters [1]; other ZKM text says over a hundred; the 2023 website presents 63 clips [3]. Different editions, different counts.
- **Obsolescence.** The CD-ROM became unplayable on current systems, which is why the ZKM and Forsythe Productions rebuilt it online [3][4].
- **No video stills here** (the photo above is of Forsythe himself) because the stills are under copyright. Watch "Point-Point Line" on the free website instead [2].

## From study to practice

This is practice. The videos do not plan a dance. They break a skill into small parts that a dancer drills alone and then recombines in real time.

There is an analytical, study-like layer too (interpretation). The white lines are drawings made *from* movement, after the fact, to make a structure visible. They are a diagram of practice, not a plan for a piece.
