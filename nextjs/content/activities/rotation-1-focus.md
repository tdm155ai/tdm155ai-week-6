We're focusing on focus. In the camera zone the skill is one thing, named by one word, and you'll practice it across every camera on the table: putting the plane of sharp focus exactly where you mean it to be, and holding it there, when the lens makes that hard.

## The skill

- **Focus**, on purpose, under difficulty. The video cameras carry Lensbabies, whose sweet spot of sharpness you place by tilting and turning the lens itself. The DSLRs carry prime lenses shot wide open, at their widest aperture, where depth of field is a few inches and everything in front of or behind the subject falls away.
- Both ways of focusing, on every camera: **manual**, by eye and hand, and **autofocus**, letting the camera decide and learning what it decides. The point is not that one is right. It is that you can't judge the camera's choice until you've made the choice yourself, and you can't make the choice well until you've seen what the camera sees.
- Why it is a good study: the result is unambiguous (sharp or not, and sharp on what), the feedback is immediate, and the skill lives in the hands and the eye. See [skill](../concepts/learning/skill.md), the capacity to notice relevant differences: here the difference between the eyelash and the ear.

## What you repeat, what varies

- One subject, one lens, a set of frames: ten, then twenty. Then change one thing and go again: the distance, the aperture, the subject moving toward you, a Lensbaby tilt. [Variation](../concepts/learning/variation.md) is what makes the reps teach something; the photographer's [test shot](../concepts/photography/test-shot.md) is the form.
- Then the same shot with autofocus, and compare. Where did the camera put the plane? Where did you?
- Rotate through the cameras. The Lensbaby and the wide-open prime make the same demand in different ways; what carries across them is the skill, which is the point of [transfer](../concepts/learning/transfer.md).

## Where the feedback comes from

- The frames themselves, side by side, on the back of the camera and then larger: a [contact sheet](../concepts/photography/contact-sheet.md). Sharp on the eye, or on the ear?
- A partner who calls sharp or soft before you look: [feedback](../concepts/learning/feedback.md) as result, then as diagnosis (you were turning the wrong way; you stopped a hair short).
- A count of attempts and hits: the [practice log](../concepts/learning/practice-log.md). Does the hit rate move in twenty frames? In fifty?

## The AI question

- Autofocus is the [black box](../concepts/learning-with-ai/black-box.md) in the room. Fast, mostly right, and silent about why. What does the camera decide for you, and what do you know once you have done it by hand? This is the week's question in miniature: what are you learning when it does it, and when you do?
- [Seeing](../concepts/photography/seeing.md): the thing that practice trains and no camera supplies. A model can generate a shot with shallow focus; it cannot tell you where, in your shot, the focus should fall.
