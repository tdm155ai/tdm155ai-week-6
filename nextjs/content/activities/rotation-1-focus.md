## The skill

- Getting a difficult lens sharp on the thing you mean, by hand, repeatedly: manual focus, shallow depth of field, a moving subject.
- Why it is a good study: the result is unambiguous (sharp or not), the feedback is immediate, and the skill is in the hands and the eye, not in a setting.

## What you repeat, what varies

- The same subject, the same lens, twenty frames; then change one thing (distance, aperture, the subject moving) and go again. See [variation](../concepts/learning/variation.md) and the photographer's [test shot](../concepts/photography/test-shot.md).
- The [one-lens assignment](../concepts/photography/assignment.md) and [bracketing](../concepts/photography/bracketing.md) are the two ways to structure the reps.

## Where the feedback comes from

- The frames themselves, side by side: a [contact sheet](../concepts/photography/contact-sheet.md).
- A partner who calls sharp or soft before you look: [feedback](../concepts/learning/feedback.md) as result, then as diagnosis.
- Keep a count of attempts and hits: the [practice log](../concepts/learning/practice-log.md).

## The AI question

- Autofocus is the [black box](../concepts/learning-with-ai/black-box.md) here. What does the camera decide for you, and what do you know once you have done it by hand?
- [Seeing](../concepts/photography/seeing.md): the thing that practice trains and the camera can't supply.
