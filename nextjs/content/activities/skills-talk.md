## The two senses of skill

- A [skill](../concepts/learning/skill.md) you build in yourself: a developed capacity to notice relevant differences and act on them. The Norse root means to discern.
- A skill you build for Claude: a folder with a `SKILL.md` that says when it applies and how to proceed. See [What is a Claude skill?](../skills/intro-to-skills.md) and the [agent skill](../concepts/learning-with-ai/agent-skill.md) entry.
- Why the two meet: writing a good skill for Claude requires knowing what good looks like, which is the first kind of skill. [Instructions vs. training](../concepts/learning-with-ai/instructions-vs-training.md) says what editing a skill file is and isn't.

## What practice is, in one pass

- [Purposeful practice](../concepts/learning/purposeful-practice.md): a target, an attempt, usable evidence, another attempt.
- [Learning vs. performance](../concepts/learning/learning-vs-performance.md): doing well in the session is not the same as having learned.
- [Deliberate practice](../concepts/learning/deliberate-practice.md) and its dispute, in a sentence.
- [What are you learning](../concepts/learning-with-ai/what-are-you-learning.md) when you do it, and when Claude does it: the [assistance effect](../concepts/learning-with-ai/assistance-effect.md) is the one piece of direct evidence.

## The first skill you write

- Pick one job from your Unit 2 project that you have done at least twice by hand.
- Write the `SKILL.md`: when it applies, the steps, what good output looks like, what to check. The [SKILL.md](../concepts/learning-with-ai/skill-md.md) entry is the shape; [origin story](../skills/origin-story.md) is a finished example.
- Run it on a new case. Note what you had to add. That note is the [study for an agent](../concepts/learning-with-ai/studies-for-an-agent.md).
