## The setup

- One image, three groups, three stages: trace and transfer the image to the block; cut the block; print it.
- Each group practices one skill only and receives the previous group's work. Nobody does the whole process.

## Why

- In old-school web development and in film, the skills of a job were distributed across people. Today they are distributed across people and AI agents. The [division of labor](../concepts/learning-with-ai/division-of-labor.md) entry is the frame.
- The hand-off is where the difficulty lives: what the next group needs to know that the last group didn't write down. See [the interface](../concepts/learning-with-ai/the-interface.md), [information hiding](../concepts/learning-with-ai/information-hiding.md), and [tacit knowledge](../concepts/learning/tacit-knowledge.md).
- The craft side: [decomposition](../concepts/learning/decomposition.md) and [part-whole practice](../concepts/learning/part-whole-practice.md), the [apprenticeship](../concepts/learning/apprenticeship.md) in which you learn one stage before the next, and film's [continuity](../concepts/film/continuity.md) records as the model of a good hand-off.

## The reflection, same three questions at every hand-off

- What did you receive, and what did you have to guess?
- What did you learn by doing your stage that the stage before you would have needed to know?
- What did you pass on, and how (the object, a note, a word)?
- Then the same questions for a hand-off to Claude: when you give a model a job, what are you handing over, and what do you get back that you have to guess about?
