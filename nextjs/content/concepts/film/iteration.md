---
title: Iteration
description: Revising a board, a plan, or a cut in response to something you found out; a changed cut is not by itself evidence that you learned.
author: claude
order: 13
---

Iteration is the second version made because of what the first one showed. A viewer watches your cut and says they did not notice the woman hesitate before answering; you move her reaction shot two seconds earlier and show it again. The storyboard redrawn after the animatic ran long, the camera diagram revised after rehearsal changed the blocking, the pickup shot added after the assembly showed a hole: all of these are iterations, and film's documents are full of them. The ScreenSkills description of an editor's work includes constructing alternatives as a routine task, not a sign that something went wrong.

The caution matters more here than anywhere in this category. The artifact changed; did the maker? A second cut that is better is weak evidence about you, because the improvement could have come from the viewer's note, the editor's instinct, or an agent's revision, and you cannot tell which from the file. "Version two is better" says nothing about what you can now do. "The listener's reaction now precedes the answer, so viewers see the hesitation" is a finding you can carry to the next scene. The difference between the two is the difference between doing well in the session and having learned, which the [learning versus performance](../learning/learning-vs-performance.md) entry explains in general. An agent in a loop can revise a cut many times; what it cannot do for you is the diagnosis.

Keep both versions. Write one sentence naming the relationship that changed and one naming what you expected. That pair is the record of an iteration; the file alone is not.
