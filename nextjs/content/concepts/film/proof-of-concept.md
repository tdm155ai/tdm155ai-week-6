A proof of concept is a short film made in service of a longer one. It shows the world, the tone, and one or two of the hard things a feature or a series would have to do, at a length someone will watch in a meeting. The Proof of Concept program run by Dirty Films with USC Annenberg funds exactly this: shorts tied to a feature or television project, with mentorship and an industry showcase at the end. The short's job is to make the larger project believable to the people who would have to pay for it.

That job makes it different from two things it resembles. It is not a sizzle reel, which is a promotional cut of material that already exists; the program described here supports shorts and does not define that form, so keep the two apart. And it is not a [study](study.md). A study has to be informative and can fail usefully; a proof of concept has to be convincing, and a convincing one can hide the fact that the problem it solved is not the problem the feature will have. Ten minutes of a world that works at ten minutes says little about whether it works at ninety. Before making one, decide whether you are testing the project or selling it, because the same footage cannot do both well.

Your Unit 2 prototype can be either. If it is a proof of concept, say what larger thing it is proof of and what it leaves unproven. If it is a study, say what question it answered. Either way the sentence beside the file is part of the work.
