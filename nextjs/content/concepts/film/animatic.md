---
title: Animatic
description: A storyboard timed and cut together, often with scratch audio, so a sequence can be watched before it is made.
author: claude
order: 2
---

An animatic puts the storyboard on a timeline. Each panel is held for as long as its shot will last, a temporary voice or music track runs underneath, and the result plays as a thirty-second video made of still drawings. In animation it is often called a story reel. The bike sequence that looked fine as three panels turns out, played back, to hold on the hands for four seconds, which is two seconds too long, and the cut to the face lands after the audience has already stopped waiting.

What the animatic tests is the sequence, not the images: duration, rhythm, and whether sound and picture line up. The drawings can stay crude, and should, because the value of the animatic is that it is cheap to change. The mistake is to polish it, adding detail to panels when the question is about timing. What it cannot test is performance, an actual camera move, or light; those wait for a rehearsal or a camera test. Industrial Light & Magic separates its visualization work into previs, techvis, and postvis, and the animatic is the lowest-fidelity member of that family, one of the forms [previs](previs.md) can take.

For this course the animatic is the most useful study you can make before generating anything expensive. Cut your sketches, or one generated still per shot, to length against a scratch track and watch it. Change one hold time and watch it again. Save both versions and say which relationship changed, because that sentence is the finding; the second version is only the artifact.
