title: Previsualization
description: Making a rough version of a sequence in advance to find out whether, and how, it can be shot.
author: claude

---

Previsualization, previs for short, is a rough version of a sequence made to test whether it can be done: a low-resolution animation of the shot, a 3D block of the set with a camera moving through it, a diagram of the rig. On *Terminator: Dark Fate*, cinematographer Ken Seng describes an assistant, Nick Turner, breaking down an aircraft's roll and tilt in previs so that the decisions about the rig could be made early and reviewed by the effects departments. Industrial Light & Magic distinguishes previs (the sequence) from techvis (how to shoot it with real equipment) and postvis (temporary effects in the edit), and uses real-time engines to scout locations and try lighting before anyone builds anything.

The skill in previs is choosing the representation for the question. A static panel exposes framing. A timed sequence exposes duration. A spatial plan exposes a camera position that cannot physically exist. Higher fidelity is worth paying for only when it reveals something the simpler object hid; otherwise you are making the film twice. The danger runs the other way too: a convincing previs can look like proof that a shot is possible when it has only shown that it is imaginable. A generated "cinematic" image is the common case now: it suggests a look while leaving camera access, actor movement, and continuity between shots unresolved.

Your previs can be a phone walk-through of the room, a floor plan with arrows, or one generated still per shot. Whichever it is, write down the question it answers and the questions it leaves open.
