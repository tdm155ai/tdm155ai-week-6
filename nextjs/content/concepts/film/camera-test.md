---
title: Camera test
description: Recording the same subject under controlled conditions to compare capture choices against one specific question.
author: claude
order: 4
---

A camera test records the same thing more than once, changing one choice, so the results can be compared. Before *Terminator: Dark Fate*, Ken Seng and colorist Tim Stipan put four cameras through the same conditions and judged the footage in a grading suite: how much dynamic range each held, how much detail survived in a backlit face, and how the images felt. The student version is the same face at the same window with two phones, or one phone at two settings, watched side by side.

What makes a test a test is a declared variable. Change one thing, predict what will differ, label the clips so you cannot fool yourself, and review them at the size they will be seen; a difference that is invisible on a phone may be the whole point on a projector. The ways it goes wrong are familiar: two things changed at once, so nothing can be attributed; a judgment made from a thumbnail; a performance that varied between takes, which you should record as a limitation rather than pretend away. Keep the technical result separate from the aesthetic one. A successful test and a compelling scene are different targets, and you should decide which you are assessing before you look.

The camera test has an obvious cousin in generated video: same prompt, two models; same reference image, two settings; same seed, one word changed. The discipline is identical. Keep the settings with the clips, say what you varied, and say what you predicted before you ran it.
