title: Dailies

Dailies, also called rushes, are the recorded footage watched during production, usually at the end of a shooting day, by the director, the cinematographer, and whoever else needs to see it. The name suggests yesterday's material and often that is what it is, but the term does not depend on it; what defines dailies is that they are reviewed while the set still stands and the actors are still under contract. On *Only God Forgives*, cinematographer Larry Smith had the dailies projected on a large screen, because a small monitor hides the focus and lighting problems a cinema will show.

Dailies are the first feedback loop in filmmaking, and the one with the shortest distance between the observation and another attempt. A soft shot seen tonight can be reshot tomorrow; the same shot seen in the edit three weeks later cannot. Two things make the review useful. The first is watching under the conditions the work will be seen in, which is why Smith wanted the big screen and why your phone is the wrong place to judge a clip meant for a projector. The second is watching with a question: performance, story, or technical, named before the lights go down. What dailies cannot tell you is whether the scene cuts together, because that needs the assembly. Lumet gives rushes their own chapter in *Making Movies* and treats watching them as part of the day's work rather than an afterthought.

Watch your own material the day you make it, at full size, with one question. For generated clips, that means the full-resolution file, not the preview thumbnail, and it means looking before you generate the next one.
