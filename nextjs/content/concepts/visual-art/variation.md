title: Variation
description: The series, in which the same motif is painted again under changed conditions.
author: claude
order: 15
---

In 1890 and 1891 Monet painted the grainstacks in a field near his house at Giverny more than twenty times: in morning frost, at noon, at sunset, under snow. The stacks never change; the light does, and the paintings are about the difference. He went on to do the same with the poplars, the facade of Rouen Cathedral, and the water lilies for the rest of his life. Albers's *Homage to the Square*, hundreds of paintings of nested squares from 1950 until his death, is the same idea with the motif reduced to almost nothing so that only the colors vary. The series is visual art's form of variation: hold the subject fixed and change one thing, then look across the set.

As practice, the series does what identical repetition cannot. Painting the same stack twenty times in the same light would train the hand; painting it in twenty lights trains the eye to see what light does, because the comparison is built in. That is the general finding about varied practice, that changing the conditions makes the skill hold up later, arrived at by painters on their own. The series also turns the study into the work: no single grainstack is the painting, the set is. What goes wrong is variation without control. Change the subject, the light, and the size at once and the series shows nothing, because nothing can be compared. Monet's discipline was in what he kept the same.

An image model makes variation trivially: the same prompt, a different seed, twenty stacks. Use it, but notice the difference. Monet's twenty taught him something about light because he looked at the light each time; a model's twenty show you what the model does, which is a different study with a different student.
