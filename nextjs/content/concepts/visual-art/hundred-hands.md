title: Hundred hands
description: A page of the same thing drawn again and again, and what such a page does and does not show.
author: claude

The hundred hands is the week's name for a page covered with one subject drawn over and over: a sheet of hands in every position, a page of eyes, forty ears. Every art student has made one, and many sheets by the masters look like one. Michelangelo's Libyan Sibyl sheet repeats the hand, the foot, the toe; Leonardo's anatomical sheets draw the same shoulder from several angles and in several layers. The page is the visual form of repetition, and the instinct to drill a hand until it comes out right is a sound one: the hand is what most drawings get wrong, and repetition with attention does make it reliable.

But a sheet of many hands is not proof that its maker drilled. Look again at the Sibyl sheet: the hand drawn three times is not the same hand three times. Each drawing asks something different, the turn of the wrist, the pressure of the fingers, and together they are a set of studies on one problem, not reps. A page can also be reference rather than practice: a hand seen once from several sides because a painting needed all of them. From the page alone you cannot tell drill from investigation from reference. What the page shows is that the artist's attention stayed on one thing; what it does not show is why, or whether anything improved between the first hand and the fortieth. Whether sheer repetition makes experts is itself disputed (Ericsson's deliberate-practice claims against later meta-analyses), so treat the page as evidence of attention, not a recipe.

Make your hundred hands this week, but write on the page what each one was for. A page of repetitions with a question beside each is a record a teacher can read, and a model cannot make for you.
