title: "Learning vs. performance"
description: "Soderstrom and Bjork's distinction: doing well in the session is not the same as having learned; the week's most useful distinction."
author: claude
order: 19
---

Two students practice a camera move. The first does the same move twenty times in a row and nails it by the end of the hour; the second mixes it with two other moves and looks worse all session. A week later the second student does the move better. Soderstrom and Bjork's review names the difference. Performance is what you can do now, during or right after practice, under the conditions you practiced in. Learning is the durable change in what you can do later, somewhere else. The two come apart more often than intuition allows: conditions that produce high performance in the session (massed repetition, constant conditions, frequent feedback) often produce less learning, and conditions that produce more learning often look worse while you are in them.

This matters for practice because you grade yourself on performance; it is what you can see. A session that feels productive may have been, or may have been the smooth feeling of doing again what you already could. The remedy is to test for learning: come back after an interval, change the conditions, remove the support, and see what is there. It matters more for this week. A polished AI-assisted artifact is performance by a particular arrangement of person and tool. It shows that the arrangement produced the result; it does not by itself show which capacity the person acquired. Only a later attempt, with the assistance changed, shows that.

For every study, plan the test for learning when you plan the study: what will you try later, without which support, and what will count as evidence?
