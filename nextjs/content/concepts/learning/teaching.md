A student can practice alone for a year and improve. What the teacher adds in an hour is a different kind of thing. The piano teacher plays the phrase so the student hears what it is supposed to sound like (demonstration). She plays it slowly, talking through the decisions, so the student sees how it is done rather than only what it is (modeling). She says "next week, the left hand alone, at half tempo" (selecting the next task, which is harder than it looks: it has to be just past what the student can do). And she says "you are rushing the third beat," naming a thing the student was doing and could not hear. These four are most of what teaching is, in every discipline here.

Each is a judgment the learner cannot yet make. Demonstration supplies the target when the learner doesn't know what good looks like. Modeling exposes the process, which a finished performance hides. Task selection is the lever the expertise research cares most about: whatever the dispute over how much practice explains, the designed task at the edge of ability is the part a learner can't design alone, because they can't see the edge. Naming is Ingold's [education of attention](education-of-attention.md). What teaching can't do is practice for the student, and what goes wrong is the lesson that substitutes explanation for attempts, so that the student leaves understanding more and able to do no more.

A model can demonstrate, model a process on request, and name things it notices in your work; what it does less well is select your next task, because it does not know what you can do. That part, this week, is the instructor's and yours.
