Close the notes and explain, from memory, why you placed the cut where you did. Before reopening the shot list, reconstruct it. Before looking at how you solved the problem last time, solve it again. Retrieval practice is using a test as a way to learn, not only to measure. Roediger and Karpicke showed that students who read a passage once and then tried to recall it remembered more a week later than students who read it several times, even though the rereaders felt more confident. Dunlosky and colleagues' review of ten learning techniques rated practice testing, with distributed practice, as the two with the strongest and broadest support.

Retrieval works because pulling something out of memory strengthens it more than putting it in again, and because a failed retrieval tells you exactly what you don't have. It does not need to be a quiz. For a maker it can be reconstructing a plan, diagnosing a deliberately flawed example, or explaining a decision before reading the explanation you wrote last week. What it requires is that the retrieval be a retrieval: recognizing a familiar explanation when it is shown to you is a different and weaker act than producing it. The limit is that you can only retrieve what was once there; retrieval practice strengthens, it does not teach the first time.

A model offers the answer the moment you ask, which removes the retrieval. The habit to build this week: say your own answer, or write it, before you read the model's. The gap between the two is the most useful feedback you'll get.
