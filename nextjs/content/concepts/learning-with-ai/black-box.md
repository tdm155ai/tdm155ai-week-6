A camera is a black box. You frame, you press, you get an image, and you do not think about the sensor, the color processing, or the people who wrote the firmware. The term comes from engineering, for any unit you treat by its inputs and outputs alone, and Bruno Latour used it for what happens to any working arrangement: as long as it functions, its parts, its history, and its makers recede, and you deal with it as one thing. A chat box is the most complete black box most of us have used. One text field in, one answer out, and the box is now the size of a media production company.

"Open the black box" sounds like one operation, but a box can hide four different things, and each needs a different kind of opening. Implementation: what the model or renderer does inside, which documentation, tests, and inspection of intermediate files partly reveal. Workflow: the sequence of steps the agent took, visible in a harness as tool calls and changed files, invisible in a chat. Judgment: why this cut or this source and not another, which needs alternatives and a reason, and which a model's explanation of itself supplies only as a plausible story. And infrastructure and labor: the services, data, and people under the output. This four-way split is this course's working frame, not Latour's. When you say you want to understand what Claude did, say which of the four, because seeing one does not show you the others.

Black boxing is not the enemy. Every skill you have sits on boxes you have never opened, and the question is never "open everything" but "which visibility does this task need." The next entry, [reopening the box](reopening-the-box.md), is about the moment that question gets forced.
