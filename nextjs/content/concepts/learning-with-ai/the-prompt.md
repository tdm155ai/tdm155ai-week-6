title: The prompt
description: The writing prompt and the model prompt share a word; what a good prompt specifies, and the prompt as a study of your own intentions.

---

In a writing class, a prompt is a constraint that starts something: "describe a room from the point of view of someone who has just received bad news in it." For a model, the prompt is the text you send: the request, the material, the constraints, the form you want back. The two uses share more than a word. Both are specifications that leave the execution open, and in both cases the quality of what comes back depends on how well the prompt names what it wants. "Make it better" is a bad writing prompt and a bad model prompt for the same reason.

A good model prompt says what the goal is (and for whom), what material to work from, what constraints hold, what the output should look like, and how you will know it is done. It often works better with an example than with an adjective. Writing one is a study of your own intentions: to tell the model what you want, you have to find out, and the first draft of the prompt usually reveals that you did not know. This is the answer to the worry that prompting is not a skill. Specifying is a skill, the same one a director uses when giving a note and a client uses when briefing a designer. It is thin if it is the only one you have, because then you can want things and cannot judge them. But it is not nothing, and the way to get better at it is the way you get better at writing: drafts, and a record of what each draft got.

A skill file is a prompt that has been studied until it is stable. The writing category's [prompt](../writing/prompt.md) is the cousin on the other side of the word.
