title: "Knowing at an interface"
description: "What you need to be able to do to say you know a thing you do through a tool: describe, inspect, perturb, recover."
author: claude
order: 13
---

You say you know how to make a character turnaround with an image model. What does that claim cover? Here is a working rubric, in four verbs. Describe: state the effect you intend, in terms specific enough to be wrong ("same face, same costume, three angles, neutral light"). Inspect: say what evidence would show whether you got it, and look at that evidence rather than at the overall impression. Perturb: predict what one change of input will do to one property of the output, make the change, and check. Recover: when the tool fails, know which layer to look at and choose a next step that is not "try again."

Notice what the rubric does not require. It does not ask you to know how a diffusion model works, or how to train one, or what the API does with your request. A filmmaker does not manufacture sensors. Full-stack knowledge is not the standard, and demanding it would make every claim to skill false. What the rubric does refuse is the polished output as sufficient evidence. An image that looks right satisfies none of the four verbs on its own. Two students can both pass with different knowledge: one with a sharper eye for what is wrong, the other with a routine for inspecting files. Both have a defensible claim, and their claims are explicit enough to compare with evidence.

This is the standard for "I learned to do X with Claude." If you can describe, inspect, perturb, and recover, you know the thing at this interface. Which is also where the knowing stops: moving to another tool, or down to the level below, is a transfer you will have to earn separately.
