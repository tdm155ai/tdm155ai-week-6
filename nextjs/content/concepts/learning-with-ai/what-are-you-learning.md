---
title: What are you learning?
description: The question for every exercise this week: what you learn when you do it, and what you learn when Claude does it.
author: claude
order: 1
---

You ask Claude for a trailer cut from your interview footage, with a title card and music, and twenty minutes later you have one that would pass. Now ask the question this week asks of every exercise: what did you learn? Making documentaries takes a lifetime to get good at, and you got one with a sentence. So are you learning to make documentaries, or learning to type the sentence? If it is the sentence, what would make you better at it than the next person, and how would either of you know?

Two answers are too easy. "Nothing" ignores that you chose the footage, judged the result, and asked for a change. "Everything" credits you with the model's work. A usable answer needs the definition of a [skill](../learning/skill.md): a developed capacity to notice relevant differences, decide, and act. Did your noticing change? Can you now see why the second cut is better, or only that it is? A polished output is evidence that the arrangement of you plus the model produced it. The distinction between [learning and performance](../learning/learning-vs-performance.md) says that doing well in the session is not the same as having learned, and a model makes every session go well.

This week, write the answer down before and after each study: what you expect to be able to do afterward that you cannot do now, and then whether you can. That is what the [practice log](../learning/practice-log.md) is for here, with one extra column for who did the work. The two deliverables, a skill built in yourself and one built for Claude, are two different answers to the question, and you should be able to say which is which.
