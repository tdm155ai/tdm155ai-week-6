title: Division of labor
description: Writing down what the student did, what the model did, and what the teacher did, because the finished work does not show the split.
author: claude

---

Suppose your short documentary is done. You shot the interviews and chose the subject. Claude cleaned the transcript, proposed a structure, and wrote the first assembly. The teacher suggested moving the best line to the top. The finished cut carries none of this. It looks the same as a cut you made alone, or one a model made alone, and in a week you will not remember which decisions were yours. This is the specific problem AI adds to learning: it obscures the division of work. What you did and what Claude did are not apparent from the result, and often not even to you.

The split is invisible because output has no provenance and chats scroll away. The danger goes beyond fairness. If the record is lost, you will later read the model's explanation of a choice as something you understood, and you will credit yourself with a skill that lives in a file. Older trades knew this. The assistant engineer keeps the session log, the script supervisor keeps the continuity notes, and nobody relies on memory for who decided what. The fix is the same here: a record with an author label on each move. Student, teacher, model. One word is enough.

This week, keep three columns for each project: the aim, the skill you are practicing yourself, and the evidence you can inspect. Beside each attempt, note who did it and what assistance was on. The record is also what lets you make the choice deliberately instead of by default: which skills stay with you, and which go into a [skill for Claude](agent-skill.md).
