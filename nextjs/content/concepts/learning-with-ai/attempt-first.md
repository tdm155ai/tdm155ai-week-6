---
title: Attempt first
description: Try, or at least diagnose, before asking; the rule that keeps the learning in the loop.
author: claude
order: 6
---

Your interview audio sounds bad. The fast move is to describe the clip to Claude and ask what is wrong. The rule here is: listen first and write one sentence. "I think the mic is too far from her and the room is doing the rest." Then ask. If the model says the same thing, you have confirmed a judgment that is now yours. If it says something else, you have a disagreement to resolve, which is where learning happens. If you asked first, you have an answer, and no way of knowing whether you could have found it.

The reasons are the ordinary ones from the Learning category, with a model making each of them sharper. An attempt is a retrieval, and retrieval is what makes a thing stick; a model removes the need to retrieve. An attempt creates a baseline, so the answer can be compared against something; without one, the answer is absorbed and feels like what you already thought. And a prediction made before the result is the one honest test of whether your judgment is improving. The experiment in [the assistance effect](assistance-effect.md) is the warning: the tutor that withheld answers mostly avoided the harm the answer-giving version caused. What goes wrong is the token attempt, a guess made so you can say you tried. The attempt has to be the real first move, with a reason attached.

Attempt-first is for skills you have chosen to keep. For a task you have decided to delegate, it is wasted effort, and knowing the difference is the week's first job. Decide, then apply the rule to the right side of the line.
