---
title: Studies for an agent
description: The small tries that teach you what to put in a skill; the étude for an agent.
author: claude
order: 26
---

Before you write a skill, do the task with the model three times by hand. Make a shot list from one folder, then another, then a hard one. Each time, note where you had to step in: the metadata it ignored, the format it drifted from, the still it described wrong, the moment you said "no, by setup, not by image." Those notes are the skill. They are also studies in the painter's sense: small pieces of work made to work one thing out, so that the finished thing can be made with the problem already solved. The painter studies the hand before the portrait; you study the task before the SKILL.md.

What the studies train is in you, not the file. You learn your own procedure, which you did not know until you watched someone else fail to follow it. You learn where the exceptions are and which ones matter. You learn what an example would say better than a sentence, and where the model needs to stop and ask instead of guessing. And you learn the shape of the box: which inputs the model's behavior is sensitive to, and which it ignores. What goes wrong is skipping the studies and writing the skill from the ideal procedure in your head, which produces a confident file that fails on the second case; or doing the studies and not recording them, so that the skill holds the conclusions and you hold nothing.

Keep the studies in the skill's folder, beside the references, and keep the hard case as the first entry in its [evaluation](evaluation.md). When the skill later fails, the studies are where you reopen it.
