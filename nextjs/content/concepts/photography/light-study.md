---
title: The light study
description: "Holding the subject and the framing still while the light changes direction, so you can see what light does."
author: claude
order: 9
---

A light study keeps everything fixed but the light. One object on a table, one camera position, and then: a window to the left, the window to the right, the window behind, a lamp from above, a white card bouncing the lamp back into the shadow side. Six frames of the same cup, and in them you can watch its form appear and disappear. Side light draws the texture and the volume; front light flattens; back light makes a silhouette with a bright rim; the card lifts the shadows so the dark side has detail again. The variables are direction, the hardness or softness of the source, contrast, and reflection, and each can be isolated the same way.

As an exercise this is the course's proposal, in the spirit of the painter's study of drapery or of a hand. We don't claim it as a documented drill of Adams or any named photographer, though studio photographers do versions of it whenever they test a setup. What it trains is the ability to read light in a room before you raise the camera, to say "hard side light from a high window" and know what it will do to a face. What it can't train is the decision about what the picture is for; light that flatters a cup may be wrong for a portrait. What goes wrong is letting two things change at once, the lamp and the camera, so the comparison means nothing.

Image generators respond to light vocabulary more reliably than to almost anything else ("soft window light from the left," "hard noon sun, deep shadows"), which makes the light study the most direct route from a photographer's habit to a specific prompt. Do it with a cup and a lamp first; then do it with a prompt.
