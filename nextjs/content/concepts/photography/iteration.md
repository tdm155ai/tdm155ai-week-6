title: Iteration
description: "Changing one thing about the capture, the selection, or the rendering for a stated reason, then looking again."

Iteration is the loop photographers run without naming it: shoot, look at the back of the camera, adjust, shoot again. The histogram shows the sky clipped, so you drop a stop; the subject's eyes are in shadow, so you move a step to the left; the print is muddy, so you expose the paper a little less. Each pass changes one thing for a reason the previous result gave you. Photography has three places this happens: in capture (settings, position, moment), in selection (which frame, in what order), and in rendering (how the chosen frame is processed or printed). An improvement in any one of them is a different kind of change.

As practice, iteration is where feedback becomes a decision. Its value depends on the reason being stated, because "I shot it again and liked the second one better" records nothing you can use next week. The cheap digital version is also its hazard: it is easy to iterate fast and learn slowly, chasing the display instead of the question. Keep the earlier versions (the point of a nondestructive catalog, which stores processing instructions beside an untouched original) so the sequence of decisions can be seen afterward. And note the distinction this week keeps returning to: an improved picture is evidence that the picture improved, not that you did.

With a generator, iteration is the main thing you do, and the warning sharpens. Re-prompting until an output pleases you is iteration; it is only a study if each re-prompt names what the previous output got wrong and what you changed. Write that down beside the prompts and the history becomes a practice log.
