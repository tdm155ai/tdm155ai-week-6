title: The test shot
description: "A frame made to answer one named question before the real shooting, and the adjustment it leads to."
author: claude

A test shot is a picture you make in order to check something, not to keep. Is the window behind her going to blow out? Will a sixtieth of a second hold the hands still? Where does the shadow of the hat fall at this angle? You frame, shoot, look at the screen or the histogram, and change a setting, and the test shot's value is the pair: the question it asked and the adjustment it produced. Every working photographer makes them constantly. The difference between a test shot and a wasted frame is whether the question was said out loud, even to yourself.

As practice, the test shot is the smallest study there is, and the habit it trains is the one this week keeps asking for: predict, then check. Say what you think the frame will do before you make it; if the screen agrees, you have learned that you can see this; if not, you have found something to learn. The hazard of the digital test shot is that it is too easy, so the looking gets outsourced to the screen and the prediction never happens, and a hundred test shots teach less than three with a stated question. Film's [camera test](../film/camera-test.md) and audio's [test recording](../audio/test-recording.md) are the same move in neighboring fields, with a difference: a camera test often compares equipment, and the audio version warns that repeated performances add their own variation. In photography the subject usually holds still, which makes the test cleaner.

A test prompt is the same thing. Before the twenty you intend to keep, one output to find out whether the model understands "backlit" the way you mean it. Write the question, then the answer.
