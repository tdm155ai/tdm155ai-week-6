title: The archive

Three things get called an archive. The first is documentation: the settings, the notes, the reasons for a selection, the sheet with marks on it. The second is accumulation: every file you have ever shot, sitting on a drive. The third is a maintained collection: the Center for Creative Photography in Arizona holds photographers' negatives, prints, letters, and recorded interviews, catalogued and cared for, so that someone can go back to the material behind a body of work. A catalog in Lightroom is a small version of the third: it stores references to your originals, the processing instructions for each, and your ratings and keywords, without touching the original file. The pile is not an archive until something organizes it.

For practice the archive matters because it is what lets you return. A study is a comparison, and comparison over time requires that the earlier attempts still exist in a form you can find. The things to keep are the originals, the selections, and the reasons, and the third is the one students skip; a folder of good pictures with no record of why they were chosen over their neighbors is accumulation. The habit is a few lines per shoot, which is this week's [practice log](../learning/practice-log.md) in a photographer's form. What goes wrong is confusing the archive with the portfolio. A portfolio is the edit shown outward; the archive is everything behind it, including the frames that lost.

Generated images are born without a record unless you make one, so the archive question is immediate: keep the prompt, the seed, the model version, the rejected outputs, and the reason for the pick. Who made which decision (you, the model, the teacher) belongs in the record too, and it is the line the course asks for.
