title: The contact sheet
description: "A roll of negatives printed side by side at actual size, so the frames can be compared and chosen from."
author: claude

A contact sheet is a print made by laying a whole roll of negatives directly on photographic paper and exposing it, so that every frame on the roll appears at its actual size, in the order it was shot. Thirty-six small pictures on one page. A photographer reads it with a loupe and a grease pencil, circling the frames worth printing and crossing out the rest. Magnum has published many of its photographers' sheets, and on Trent Parke's sheet from Bondi Beach in 2000 you can see the chosen frame sitting among its neighbors, the near-misses a fraction of a second on either side. Digital tools reproduce the same thing as a grid of thumbnails, and the function is the same: comparison.

What the sheet teaches is that a photograph is a choice made among attempts, and that the attempts are evidence. Seen alone, a picture looks inevitable; seen on the sheet, you can tell what the photographer was working toward, where the framing tightened, when the subject turned. For your own work it is the fastest way to find out what you repeat without noticing. Two cautions. The sheet holds the frames it holds: not the other rolls, not the files you deleted, not what you never shot, so "a record of every attempt" overstates it unless you say what your sheet includes. And the sheet is itself a print with its own exposure, so the thumbnails are not neutral; a dark sheet makes every frame look underexposed.

This week, make one for whatever you make, generated images included: twenty outputs from one prompt family in a grid, the chosen one marked, with a line saying what the grid contains and leaves out. The sheet makes your selection visible to a crit, and to you.
