title: The daily photo

The daily photo is the simplest assignment there is: one picture a day, every day, for a month or a year. It has many names (a 365, a photo a day) and one mechanism, which is that the routine removes the decision about whether to shoot and leaves only the decision about what. Trent Parke, the Magnum photographer, has described photographing a possum that jumped outside his parents' house every night for thirty nights while he waited for his son to be born. The account establishes sustained return to one subject, which is a real thing and a photographer's habit. It does not establish that each night was a drill, or that night thirty was measurably better than night one.

That is the caution to carry into your own routine. A daily practice keeps the eye in use, lowers the cost of a bad day, and over months builds the archive that the series and the contact sheet work from; the writing version is the daily pages in [routine](../writing/routine.md). But doing a thing every day is a condition for improvement, not evidence of it, and the trap of the daily photo is mistaking the streak for the skill. Without a question and without review it becomes a diary of snapshots. The fix is small: pair the routine with one of the other studies here, so that for a week the daily photo is a light study and for the next it is one block, and look at the month on a sheet before deciding whether anything changed.

With a generator, the daily version is a daily prompt, and the same test applies. Thirty days of outputs in a grid will show you whether your prompts got more specific, which is the skill, or whether the model just kept being good.
