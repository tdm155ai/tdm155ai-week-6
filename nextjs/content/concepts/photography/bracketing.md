---
title: Bracketing
description: "Making the same picture several times while one setting changes in set steps above and below a starting point."
author: claude
order: 4
---

To bracket is to shoot the same frame three, five, or seven times, stepping one setting in fixed increments on either side of what the meter suggests: a stop under, the meter's reading, a stop over. Most often the setting is shutter speed, with aperture held so that depth of field doesn't change between frames; cameras can also bracket white balance or flash. The original purpose was insurance on film, when you couldn't see the result until the lab. The study purpose is sharper. A bracket is a controlled comparison, where everything between neighboring frames is identical except the thing you chose to vary and the amount you varied it by.

That makes bracketing the strictest form of variation photography has, and the one to learn first. It differs from "take a bunch and pick later," because the variable and the interval are declared in advance, and it differs from HDR, which merges the bracket into one image rather than comparing its members. What it trains is the link between a number and an effect: you learn what a stop looks like, in this light, on this subject. What it can't train is the choice of what to bracket. What goes wrong is automation. If the camera's auto modes quietly shift ISO to compensate, your three frames did not vary what you think they varied, so record what changed.

Bracketing transfers directly to generation. Hold the prompt and the seed, step one parameter (guidance, a single adjective, the stated focal length) in even increments, and lay the results out in order. The grid shows what the knob does, which a single output never can.
