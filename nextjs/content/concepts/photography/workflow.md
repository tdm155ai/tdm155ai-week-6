title: Workflow

A photographer's workflow is the order of operations from the moment of exposure to the finished thing: capture, then organization (importing, naming, keywording, backing up), then selection, then processing of the selected frames, then output, a print or a file sized for a screen. Every stage has its own tools and its own decisions, and each is a place where a study can be made. In the film era the stages were physical and hard to skip, exposing, developing, contact-printing, choosing, enlarging; the digital version collapses them into one program and makes it easy to forget they are different jobs.

Naming the stages is useful for practice because it lets you decompose. A student who says "my pictures are bad" has not said which stage is failing, and the stages fail differently: capture problems show up as exposure and focus, selection problems as a strong set with a weak pick, processing problems as a good frame made worse. Isolating one stage and drilling it (a week of selection on old material, no shooting) is the part-whole move from [decomposition](../learning/decomposition.md). The caution is that the stages loop. Output reveals a selection mistake; a print reveals that the capture needed another stop; the sequence is a description, not a law. The [film](../film/workflow.md) and [audio](../audio/workflow.md) workflows describe the same thing with their own handoffs.

A generated image has a workflow too: prompt, generate, organize, select, upscale or edit, output. Writing yours down, stage by stage, with what you decide at each one and what the model decides, is both a study of your own procedure and the first draft of a skill file, the instructions that would let an agent run the stages you are willing to hand over.
