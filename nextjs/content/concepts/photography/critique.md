title: Critique
description: "Other people's observations of your pictures, tied to what you were trying to do and to the next experiment."
author: claude
order: 13
---

A crit is a room where your prints are on the wall and other people say what they see. The useful version follows a shape. You say what you were after; they say what they notice first and what the picture does to them; then the conversation tries to connect the two, and ends with something you could try next. ICP's crit lab asks participants to bring a body of work in progress, to talk about their goals, influences, and difficulties, and to keep a notebook for the feedback they get; the point is a cohesive body of work and a clearer sense of your own aims, not a verdict. "The left one is better" is not a crit. "In the left one my eye goes to the window and never reaches her face; was that what you wanted?" is.

Critique is where photography's feedback stops being self-administered. The contact sheet shows you your attempts; the crit shows you what you can't see in them, because you remember the morning and the viewer doesn't. What it trains is the distance that selection also needs, and the habit of stating an aim before you can be told whether you reached it. What goes wrong is treating a teacher's preference as the answer, which makes the crit a test of taste, or taking the note without a next step, which makes it a review. The general account, feedback as outcome, diagnosis, and intervention, is in [Learning](../learning/feedback.md); the writing [workshop](../writing/workshop.md) is the nearest sibling.

You can ask a model to crit your pictures, and it will say plausible things about the rule of thirds. It cannot know what you were after unless you tell it, and once you have told it, the useful part has mostly happened. Bring the sheet and the aim to people first.
