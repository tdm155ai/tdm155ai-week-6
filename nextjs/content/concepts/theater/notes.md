title: Notes
description: The director's feedback after a run, delivered as a list, written down by the actors, and tried the next time.

After a run-through the cast sits on the stage floor and the director reads from a notebook. "Page 42, the cross to the window is late, go on 'anymore.' Second act, you're playing the end of the scene from the beginning; he hasn't lied yet. The laugh is landing on 'tomorrow,' so wait for it." The actors write the notes down. The convention in many rooms is that you do not argue with a note and you do not explain yourself; you say thank you, and you take the note, which means you try it the next time you run.

Notes are feedback from outside, and theater needs them because an actor cannot see the scene she is in. A good note is a diagnosis attached to something playable: not "be sadder" (a result the actor cannot act) but "try to make him stay" (an action she can). The split between result notes and action notes is a piece of craft directors learn, and the research on feedback describes it in other words: outcome, diagnosis, intervention. What goes wrong is volume (forty notes after one run, most of them lost), notes given before the actor has the lines and blocking to use them, and the actor who performs the note, visibly doing what she was told so that the correction shows.

A model can take notes on a recording of a run and some of them will be fair. What it does not have is the director's knowledge of what was intended and what was tried last week, which is what makes a note a step in a process rather than a review. A useful constraint for any such tool: observable actions first (she turned away on the line), interpretation second (she seemed angry), and the two kept apart.
