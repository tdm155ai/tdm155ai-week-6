---
title: Understudy
description: Learning a role by watching someone else rehearse and play it, and being ready to go on with hours of notice.
author: claude
order: 13
---

The understudy sits in the back of the rehearsal room with a script, writing down the blocking as it is set, and in the wings during the run, watching the actor she covers. She rarely rehearses the role on the stage with the full company; understudy rehearsals happen on the set during the day, with the stage manager and whoever else is covering, and in a long run maybe once a week. Then one evening at five the call comes, and at eight she is on. In musicals, swings cover several ensemble tracks at once and may go on in a different one each night.

The understudy learns a role as a finished set of decisions she did not make: this cross, this pause, this reading. It is learning by observation and copying, the worked example in its purest theatrical form, and it is a real skill. An understudy has to absorb a performance from outside, hold it without practicing it much, and reproduce it well enough that the other actors' cues and blocking still work. What it cannot give her is her own choices, or the weeks of scene work in which the choices were made; she inherits the map without having walked it. Understudies who go on sometimes report that the role only became theirs after a few performances, when the copy began to be replaced by something found live.

The understudy's chair is the one a recording tool, or a model, can sit in: watching, writing down what was decided, keeping the record so someone absent can reconstruct it. The week's question applies with unusual clarity. The understudy who has only watched knows the role in one sense and not in another, and only the night she goes on reveals which.
