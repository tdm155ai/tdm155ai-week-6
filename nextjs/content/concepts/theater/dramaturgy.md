---
title: Dramaturgy
description: Research as part of the production, carried by the dramaturg, who studies the play's world and brings it into the rehearsal room.
author: claude
order: 15
---

Before the first rehearsal of a play set in 1920s Vienna, someone has assembled a packet: a glossary of every reference in the script, what the money was worth, how people addressed each other, what the newspapers said that spring, the playwright's other work, and what earlier productions did with the scene everyone finds hard. That someone is the dramaturg. During rehearsal the dramaturg sits in the room, answers questions ("would she have been allowed to say that to him?"), and watches the structure from the outside while the director is inside it. The role has a long history; its name is usually traced to Lessing's writing on the Hamburg theater in the 1760s, and the production dramaturg in the sense above is more recent.

Dramaturgy is the research strand of a making process, and it is a study in the sense this week means: work done in order to work something out, most of which the audience never sees. It trains a kind of reading, close and practical, where every question about the text is a question about what to do on Wednesday. What goes wrong is research that stays in the packet, forty pages nobody in the cast reads, or research that becomes a cage, so that a true detail about 1920s Vienna wins over a choice that would make the scene play. The dramaturg's judgment is about which facts matter to the room.

A model is a fast dramaturg and sometimes a careless one. It will produce the glossary, the historical context, and a plausible answer to the question about Vienna, and some fraction of it will be invented with the same confidence as the rest. The dramaturg's job does not disappear; it moves to checking, which requires knowing enough to notice what is wrong.
