title: Iteration

Iteration is revision seen as a series: version one, version two, version seven, each made in response to what the last one showed. Walt Whitman's papers at the Library of Congress hold trial titles, trial lines, notes, and drafts of poems, and *Leaves of Grass* went through several revised editions in his lifetime. The surviving pages make the workshop visible instead of letting the finished poem stand for the whole process. For a student the form is simpler: a folder with `opening-v1.md`, `opening-v2.md`, `opening-v3.md`, and one line in a log for each, saying what it tried.

What iteration trains is the habit of treating each version as a test of something, which keeps revision from turning into endless fiddling. What it can't do is tell you why a change happened. A visible deletion in a Whitman draft shows that a line was cut; it doesn't show why, and students reading drafts, their own or anyone's, should use calibrated language: this version removes the detail, it appears to shift the emphasis, the reason can't be established from the page. That caution is also a skill for reading a model's account of its own process. The failure mode is the pile of versions nobody reads back; without the log line, versions are only files.

With a model in the loop, iteration gets fast and the record gets thin. An agent will produce a changed artifact every time you ask, and a changed artifact is not evidence that you learned anything; the version count goes up and your judgment may not. Keep the log with attribution: which versions were yours, which were the model's, and what you decided between them.
