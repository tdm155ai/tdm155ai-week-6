title: Repetition
description: The combination done again, and then again on the other side.

The teacher sets a sixteen-count combination of tendus, the class does it on the right side, and then, without discussion, does it on the left. If it fell apart, the room does it again. In rehearsal the same thing happens at a larger scale: "from the top of the phrase" is said dozens of times in an afternoon, and a company can run a two-minute section twenty times before lunch. Repetition is the texture of a dancer's day, and most of a dancer's life is spent doing something for the second, fiftieth, or thousandth time.

What makes it work is that dance repetition is almost never identical. The second side is a different problem for a body that prefers one side. The next run has a correction in it ("finish the plié this time"), or is slower, or has the music, or has the music taken away. Something is held fixed and something is changed, and the dancer knows which. The failure mode is the other kind: running the phrase again with nothing changed, hoping it gets better on its own. The body learns whatever it does most, including the mistake, and the studio rule is blunt: don't practice the mistake. Stop, fix the thing, then repeat. The [general account](../learning/repetition.md) of why repetition helps and when it bakes in errors applies to dance with unusual force, because the record of the error is the body itself and takes weeks to rewrite.

When you ask a model to regenerate a clip ten times, repetition is happening, but it is happening to the artifact, not to you. The dancer's version of the question for the week is simple: after the tenth run, what has changed in your body, and what has changed only in the file?
