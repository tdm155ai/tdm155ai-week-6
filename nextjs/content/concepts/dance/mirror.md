title: Mirror
description: The studio's built-in feedback, where you see yourself as the teacher sees you, as long as you face front.
author: claude
order: 8
---

A dancer looks up, sees that her arm is higher than everyone else's, and lowers it an inch. The mirror on the studio's front wall is the one piece of feedback equipment every dance studio has, and it answers the dancer's basic problem: she cannot feel her own shape accurately. The arm that feels level is not; the leg that feels turned out is turned in. The mirror shows the outside, which is what the teacher and the audience see and the dancer never does.

Its limits are as well known as its uses. It works only facing front, and most choreography does not. It is flat and reversed. It trains the eye to do a job the body should learn to do itself, and a dancer who checks every balance in the glass is lost on stage, where there is no mirror and the lights hide the front row. So teachers turn the class to face the back wall, or hang a curtain, or make dancers close their eyes for a balance, to force the correction to come from sensation instead of the picture. The subtler problem is that the mirror lets you fix the image without ever learning what the correct position feels like, and only the feeling travels with you.

Video is a mirror with memory, and an AI pose tool is a mirror with opinions. Both give feedback on the image and neither gives feedback on the sensation, which is fine as long as you know which one you are calibrating. The dancer's practice offers a clear test: turn away from it and see what is still there.
