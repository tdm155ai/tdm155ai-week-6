---
title: Répétiteur
description: The rehearsal director who carries the dance in memory and teaches it into other bodies.
author: claude

---

When a company stages a Balanchine ballet, a répétiteur arrives who learned it from dancers who learned it from Balanchine, and teaches it count by count, from memory, correcting each body against the version she carries. The word is French: in French a rehearsal is a répétition, and the répétiteur is the one who runs it. In ballet the job is also called ballet master or rehearsal director; in modern companies it may be a former dancer of the role. The Cunningham Trust's authorized teachers hold the same position for that technique. Whatever the title, the person is the dance's memory and its means of transmission.

The répétiteur is the feedback loop of rehearsal. She holds the standard version, teaches it, watches for drift, and decides when it is right, and her existence says something about the field: dance lives in people, not in files, so it needs a profession of people who carry it. What goes wrong is what goes wrong with any chain of memory. Each transmission changes the piece a little, so a ballet staged by two répétiteurs is two ballets; and the répétiteur's own body tends to become the standard, so a role gets rebuilt around the proportions and habits of the person who last danced it. Companies argue about which version is the real one, and notation and video exist partly to referee.

An archive of videos is not a répétiteur; it cannot see you and cannot correct you. The comparison to a skill file is close. A written procedure is a record of how, and it still needs someone in the room who knows what right looks like and can tell when the record is being followed wrong.
