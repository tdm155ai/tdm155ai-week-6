---
title: Phrase
description: The combination or movement phrase, the unit a dance is built from and the unit a dancer learns.
author: claude
order: 6
---

A phrase of movement is a sequence with a beginning and an end, like a sentence: a few eights long, with a rise and a fall in it, something that can be done on its own and still looks like dance. In class it is called a combination, and the teacher sets a new one for every exercise. In choreography it is called a phrase, and the choreographer makes it, names it ("the falling phrase," "the one after the lift"), and hands it to dancers who learn it as a unit. "Take it from the lift phrase" is how a rehearsal is navigated.

The phrase is the unit because it fits. It is small enough to hold in the body all at once and large enough to have dynamics, a breath, a shape in time; a single step has none of that, and a whole dance is too much to learn in one piece. Learning in phrases is the dancer's form of chunking, and the place memory fails is predictable: the seam. "I know both halves, I just can't get from one to the other" is said in every rehearsal, and the join needs its own practice. For a choreographer the phrase is also the raw material. A phrase made once gets varied: done backward, inverted, slowed, given to three dancers in canon, cut in half and spliced to another. Much of a long dance is a few phrases under transformation.

When you ask a model for a dance, in text or in video, the phrase is what you have to be able to specify, and you can only specify what you can see in units. A student who cannot find the phrases in a clip of dancing cannot tell the model what is wrong with its version.
