"Your hip is lifting on the développé." Or no words at all: the teacher walks over, presses the hip down with one hand, and the dancer feels where it is supposed to be. In dance the word for feedback is the correction, and corrections are the main thing a teacher produces; a class with no corrections is a class without a teacher. They are also public. A correction is given to one dancer and meant for the room, and everyone applies it, which is why a crowded class still teaches each person in it.

The [general account](../learning/feedback.md) sorts feedback into outcome, diagnosis, and intervention, and the athletics version separates knowing the result from knowing how the movement went. Dance corrections are almost all the second kind: about how, not whether. "That was late" is an outcome; "because you didn't finish the plié" is a diagnosis; "deeper plié, and go again" is the intervention, and the good teacher gives all three in one breath and watches the next attempt. Hands-on correction carries information words cannot, where a limb should be, how much weight is enough, and it is now negotiated with consent in many studios rather than assumed. What goes wrong: too many corrections at once, so none lands; a correction applied as a new error, the hip now too far the other way; and the "noted" nod from a body that has not changed. A correction has not happened until the next attempt is different.

A model can watch a clip of you and produce an outcome and a plausible diagnosis, and the diagnosis will be fluent whether or not it is right; it has not felt your hip from inside. Write down what you observed before you write down why. That order is the whole discipline of being corrected.
