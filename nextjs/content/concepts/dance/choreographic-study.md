---
title: Choreographic study
description: A short piece made to try one idea, a phrase, a structure, a constraint, before or instead of a whole dance.
author: claude
order: 18
---

A composition class is given an assignment: make a one-minute study in canon, or a study using only the arms, or a study in which no one leaves the floor. Each student makes it, shows it, and the class talks about it. Doris Humphrey's *The Art of Making Dances* works this way, laying out the devices of composition, among them repetition, contrast, and the shape of a phrase, as problems to be tried in small pieces. Working choreographers do the same thing in private: two dancers, one phrase, an afternoon in a studio, to find out whether an idea moves before anyone commits a company to it.

The study separates composition from execution. Structure, time, space, and the relations among bodies can be worked on while the dancing itself is rough, and a study is cheap enough to throw away, which is what makes it safe to try something that may not work. It is the dance form of the painter's [study](../visual-art/study.md) and the musician's [étude](../music/etude.md): one problem isolated so it can be seen. What it cannot do is stand in for the whole. An idea that works for a minute may collapse at ten, a device that is interesting once is tiresome the third time, and the study's success is about its question, not about how well it was danced, which students confuse in both directions, dismissing a telling study because it was scrappy and admiring a polished one that found out nothing.

Asking a model for twenty variations of a phrase in video is a study of the model, not of composition, unless you have set the problem first. The study's discipline is naming the one thing you are trying to find out, and that part does not delegate.
