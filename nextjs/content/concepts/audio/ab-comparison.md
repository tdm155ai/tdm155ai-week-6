---
title: A/B comparison
description: Switching between two defined versions of the same excerpt, at the same level, to hear what differs.
author: claude
order: 6
---

Loop eight seconds of your mix and flip the compressor on the voice track in and out. With it is A, without it is B, and the comparison is the switch. An A/B works when the two sides are the same excerpt, at the same playback level, in the same part of the piece, with one difference between them. Level matters more than people expect: louder sounds better to most listeners, so an unmatched comparison mostly tells you which version is louder. The iZotope tutorial on reference tracks makes the same point about comparing a mastered record to your working mix: turn the reference down first.

Know what the method is not. A/B describes comparing two alternatives; it does not mean blind, randomized, or statistically meaningful. You know which is which, and you know which one you spent the afternoon on. A blind test, where someone else flips and the labels are hidden, is stricter; an ABX test, which adds a third clip that is either A or B and asks you which, is stricter still; the formal listening-test standard adds trained listeners, references, controlled rooms, and rest periods. Your flip test is informal, and still useful, as long as you do not call it proof. A loudness match also does not settle whether two different passages are perceptually equivalent; it removes one confound, not all of them.

Keep a line in the log for each comparison: the excerpt, what differs, the playback setting, whether it was sighted or blind, and how sure you were. Keep the three questions separate, detect, describe, prefer. Then have a classmate flip without telling you which is which. The number of times you cannot tell is the lesson, and it is one you will not get from a model's description of the two files.
