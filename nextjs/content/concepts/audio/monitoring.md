A mix that sounded balanced on your laptop has no bass at all in the car. Nothing changed in the file; the laptop speakers cannot reproduce the bass, so you never heard it while you were making decisions about it. Monitoring is the playback chain you judge through: headphones or speakers, their frequency response, the room they are in, and your position relative to them (the usual advice puts you and the two speakers at the points of an equilateral triangle). Genelec's placement guidance describes how early reflections and the speaker's distance from a wall change what reaches your ears, and that some of those changes cannot be corrected with equalization.

What monitoring trains is knowledge of your own system's lies. Every setup has them, and the practice is to learn them with [reference tracks](reference-track.md) you know on other systems, to check a mix on three systems before calling it done, and to keep the playback level consistent and moderate, since louder sounds better and a level change between A and B will decide the comparison for you. Write the playback setting in the log beside every judgment. None of this is a claim that buying particular speakers improves learning; it is a claim that you should know what the ones you have are doing.

The larger point is the one the week keeps returning to: there is no unmediated judgment. The meter, the model, and the headphones each put something between you and the sound. A model can analyze the file and tell you the bass is there; it cannot know what your headphones do to it. The dancer's [mirror](../dance/mirror.md) is the same kind of instrument, built-in feedback that shows some things and hides others, and the chat box is another ([the interface](../learning-with-ai/the-interface.md)).
