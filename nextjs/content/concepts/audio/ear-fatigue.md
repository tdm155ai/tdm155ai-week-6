---
title: Ear fatigue
description: The practical limit on how long you can judge sound reliably in one stretch, and the habit of resting before deciding.
author: claude
order: 20
---

After two hours on the mix, the voice sounds fine. The next morning it is harsh and the music is buried, and the file has not changed. Ear fatigue is the everyday name engineers give to that loss of reliable judgment over a long session. The formal listening-test standard takes it seriously enough to design around: ITU-R BS.1116 warns that listener fatigue can undermine the results of a test and builds rest between sessions into the procedure.

The word bundles several things that this entry will not pretend to sort out: attention drifting, the ear adapting to whatever it has been hearing for an hour, and the separate matter of hearing damage from loud or long exposure, which is a health question and not a mixing one. No single mechanism is claimed here, and no medical advice is given. The practical rule does not need the mechanism. Keep sessions short. Keep the playback level moderate. Take breaks, and come back cold before you make the big decisions. When you return, play a [reference track](reference-track.md) before your own mix, so that the first thing you hear is not the thing you have adapted to. Treat any judgment made in hour three as provisional and write the time in the log beside it.

As a rule for designing studies, this is why a session of twenty [placements](mic-placement.md) beats a hundred in a row, and why spreading the listening across days works better than one long evening; the general account is under [spacing](../learning/spacing.md), and what you can do at the end of a long session is not what you have learned. A model does not tire, which is a reason to hand it the long mechanical passes and keep your fresh ears for the decisions that need them.
